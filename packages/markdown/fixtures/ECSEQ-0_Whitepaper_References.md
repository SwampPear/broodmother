## Claims the literature does not support

*Both were previously written as though sourced. The text now states the gap explicitly; do not quietly re-strengthen this wording.*

- **Molecular axis, Au high-frequency dielectric readout (§4D).** No source demonstrates that per-base dipole coupling is resolvable in the 10 kHz–100 kHz impedance band at a gold electrode. The two ends are supported — bases differ in dipole moment [29] and DNA on gold shifts interfacial impedance [30] — but the step between them is not. This is ECSEQ's weakest-supported physical claim.
- **Faradaic band assignment, 20–90 Hz (§4D).** No source ties the charge-transfer arc to this band; the time constant is system-specific, so no universal band exists. Separately, the DNA-on-gold EIS literature measures hybridization through a solution redox mediator, while the faradaic axis proposes direct mediator-free base oxidation — conventionally measured by DC voltammetry, not AC impedance. That is a paradigm mismatch, not just a missing citation.

- **"Regenerative Strategy of Gold Electrodes for Long-Term Reuse of Electrochemical Biosensors"** (*ACS Omega*, PMC9835648) — **RETRACTED** (notice: *ACS Omega* 2025 Oct 9; 10 (41): 49272). Do not cite under any circumstances. It surfaces readily in searches for the surface-regeneration claim.
- **Mahmoud et al. (2014)**, *Biosensors and Bioelectronics* 56: 328–333 — real, but uses full reductive desorption and reassembly of the sensor stack, not a SAM-preserving strip. Mechanism mismatch.
- **Dressman et al. (2003)**, *PNAS* 100 (15): 8817–8822 — real, discusses Poisson bead occupancy qualitatively but states no explicit ceiling percentage.
- **Pathania & Kapoor (2023)**, *Mater. Sci. Eng. B* 288: 116152 — real, but a DFT study of the *optical-frequency* dielectric function (eV-scale electronic transitions), a different physical regime from Hz–kHz impedance. It does not support the molecular axis.
