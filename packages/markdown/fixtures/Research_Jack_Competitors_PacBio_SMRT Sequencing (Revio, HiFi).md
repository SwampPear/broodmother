# SMRT Sequencing (Revio, HiFi) — PacBio

Status: **currently selling** (Revio is the current flagship; Sequel IIe sales ended 2025-11-28, support continues to 2028-09-30; Vega is a newer, lower-throughput system in the current lineup — see caveats below). Researched/compiled 2026-07-18.

## Pipeline: hair strand → SMRTbell library → real-time optical signal

1. Hair is collected with the root bulb attached (bulb follicular cells carry nuclear DNA); the shaft alone carries essentially no usable nuclear DNA.
2. Cell lysis (detergent + proteinase K) and column/bead purification of genomic DNA — but for long-read sequencing, extraction must be gentler than for short-read prep: aggressive vortexing/pipetting shears high-molecular-weight (HMW) DNA, which directly caps the achievable read length downstream. This is a first physical divergence from the short-read platforms in this vault: SMRT sequencing's entire value proposition depends on preserving long, intact input molecules.
3. DNA is quantified (fluorometric) and sized (pulsed-field gel or similar) to confirm HMW integrity — a QC step short-read platforms don't need to emphasize, since they fragment DNA down to a few hundred bp regardless of input length.
4. Genomic DNA is **mechanically** sheared (e.g., on a Diagenode Megaruptor-class instrument) to a target size — commonly ~15–20 kb for standard HiFi libraries, or left minimally sheared for ultra-long/structural-variant-focused libraries. Mechanical shearing is used instead of enzymatic tagmentation because tagmentation (Illumina-style Tn5) cuts DNA down to short (~300–500 bp) fragments, which would defeat the purpose of long-read sequencing.
5. Fragment ends are enzymatically repaired and A-tailed, then **hairpin adapters** are ligated to both ends of each double-stranded fragment. Because a hairpin adapter loops back and connects the top and bottom strands, the ligation product is a single continuous, topologically circular molecule — a **SMRTbell** template — rather than a linear double-stranded fragment with two separate adapter ends (contrast with Illumina/MGI/Element, where both ends stay open, single-stranded, and separately addressable).
6. Size selection (bead-based) removes short fragments and adapter dimers, which would otherwise waste zero-mode-waveguide (ZMW) capacity on low-value short inserts.
7. A sequencing polymerase and primer are bound to each SMRTbell template in solution, *before* loading onto the instrument — this is a discrete, off-instrument complex-formation step, not something that happens on the flow cell as in bridge-PCR or DNB-loading workflows.
8. The polymerase–template complexes are loaded onto a **SMRT Cell**: a nanofabricated array of **zero-mode waveguides (ZMWs)** — sub-wavelength-diameter holes in a thin metal film deposited on a glass substrate (25 million ZMWs per SMRT Cell on Revio; 8 million on the older Sequel IIe). At the loading concentration used, most ZMWs receive close to one polymerase–template complex each (Poisson loading, same statistical principle as flow-cell/bead loading on other platforms, but the "site" here is a physical nanophotonic structure, not a chemically functionalized spot).
9. Unlike every cyclic platform in this vault (Illumina, MGI, Element, Ultima, Ion Torrent), SMRT sequencing has **no wash-and-image cycles at all** — it runs continuously in real time. Fluorescently **phospholinked** nucleotides (the dye is attached to the terminal phosphate, not the base) diffuse freely in the bulk solution above the ZMW array. A ZMW's sub-wavelength aperture prevents propagating light from illuminating the bulk solution above it, so only nucleotides transiently held in the immobilized polymerase's active site at the *bottom* of the ZMW — a zeptoliter-scale observation volume — produce a detectable fluorescence pulse against the dark background.
10. When the polymerase binds and incorporates the correct nucleotide, the dye-bearing phosphate is cleaved away as a natural part of the polymerase's own catalytic cycle (phosphodiester bond formation releases pyrophosphate, carrying the dye with it) — so no separate chemical cleavage step is needed before the next base, and the DNA product itself is never dye-labeled or otherwise chemically modified by the sequencing process. A camera continuously records each ZMW's fluorescence-pulse train and calls a base each time a pulse of the correct color/duration is detected.
11. Because the template is a circular SMRTbell, the strand-displacing polymerase reads around the same molecule repeatedly, generating several independent **subreads** covering both strands of the original fragment (the number of passes depends on insert length vs. run time — shorter inserts get more passes in a fixed run).
12. On-instrument software aligns and collapses the multiple subreads from one ZMW into a single **circular consensus sequence (CCS)** — a **HiFi read** — for that molecule. Because each subread's errors are largely independent (they arise from different single-molecule photophysical/kinetic events on different passes), consensus over multiple passes drives the per-read error rate down sharply compared to any one subread — this is where HiFi's headline accuracy comes from, not from amplification-based consensus (there is no amplification in this workflow).
13. HiFi reads — each representing one original long DNA molecule, not a PCR-copied population — are aligned to a reference or used directly for de novo assembly, structural-variant calling, and haplotype phasing. A byproduct of real-time kinetics: the *timing* between successive nucleotide incorporations (inter-pulse duration) is measurably altered by certain base modifications (e.g., 5-methylcytosine), so methylation calls can be made directly from the native, unconverted DNA molecule in the same run — no bisulfite conversion step, unlike short-read methylation workflows.

## Platform profile

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sequencing chemistry | Real-time, continuous single-nucleotide incorporation by a strand-displacing DNA polymerase using phospholinked fluorescent nucleotides — not a cyclic-reversible-termination chemistry; no wash/cleave cycle. |
| Detection method | Optical — camera imaging of per-ZMW fluorescence pulses in real time as incorporation happens, confined to a sub-diffraction observation volume by the ZMW's waveguide geometry. |
| Amplification method | **None.** Each ZMW sequences one native single molecule directly; this is the platform's core physical distinguishing feature versus every other technology in this vault except Oxford Nanopore. |
| Array architecture | SMRT Cell: a nanofabricated array of ZMWs (sub-wavelength apertures in a metal film on glass) — 25,000,000 ZMWs/cell (Revio), 8,000,000 (Sequel IIe). Revio runs up to 4 SMRT Cells in parallel per instrument. |
| Typical applications | Long-read/ultra-long-read WGS, de novo genome assembly, structural-variant detection, haplotype phasing, native (non-bisulfite) methylation calling, full-length isoform/transcript sequencing (Iso-Seq), rare-disease WGS, complex/repetitive-region resolution. |
| Major advantages | Highest consensus accuracy of any long-read platform (HiFi, ~99.9%+ per-read); no PCR means no amplification-driven GC bias or duplicate reads; native epigenetic (methylation) detection without bisulfite conversion; long native molecules directly resolve structural variants, repeats, and phased haplotypes that short reads cannot span. |
| Major limitations | Requires high-molecular-weight, carefully handled input DNA — degraded or highly fragmented samples (e.g., FFPE tissue) perform poorly; run time is fixed near 24 h regardless of urgency (no rapid/portable mode); cost per Gb remains higher than short-read platforms even after recent chemistry improvements; lower raw throughput per dollar than short-read platforms for applications that only need SNV calls, not structural context. |
| Regulatory/clinical positioning | Predominantly a research/LDT (laboratory-developed test) and population-genomics platform (e.g., used within the NIH *All of Us* long-read pilot). No FDA-cleared PacBio sequencing instrument was identified in this research pass — labeled **unavailable/not found**, medium confidence this reflects reality given long-read platforms generally lag short-read platforms in formal regulatory clearance. |

## Application specialization and physical basis

- **Long-read and ultra-long-read WGS, de novo assembly, repetitive-region resolution**: this is SMRT's core strength — because there is no amplification and reads directly reflect native molecule length (15–20 kb mean, longer with size-selected libraries), SMRT reads routinely span repeat elements, segmental duplications, and structural-variant breakpoints that short reads (150–300 bp) cannot bridge.
- **Haplotype phasing**: long reads spanning multiple heterozygous variants directly link them onto the same physical molecule, enabling phasing without needing statistical population-reference phasing (a requirement for most short-read workflows).
- **Native methylation/epigenetics**: inter-pulse duration kinetics directly reveal certain base modifications during the same sequencing run — a unique capability among platforms in this vault, shared in spirit (though not mechanism) with Oxford Nanopore's direct modification-calling; both avoid destructive bisulfite conversion, unlike short-read EM-seq/bisulfite workflows.
- **GC bias / amplification bias**: absent, because there is no PCR step in the sequencing chemistry itself (library-prep steps upstream, e.g. some amplicon-based Iso-Seq workflows, can still introduce PCR if used, but standard WGS SMRTbell prep is PCR-free).
- **Homopolymer and substitution errors**: individual subread errors are dominated by stochastic single-molecule kinetic noise (missed or extra incorporations, base misidentification) rather than the phasing/dephasing error accumulation that limits SBS read length, or the pH-integration ambiguity that limits Ion Torrent homopolymer calling — but because CCS consensus averages multiple independent passes over the *same* molecule, the final HiFi per-base error rate is substantially lower than any individual subread's error rate, including in homopolymers.
- **Poor fit**: degraded/fragmented input (e.g., FFPE tumor tissue, forensic samples, cell-free DNA) — SMRT's value proposition depends on long, intact input molecules, so it underperforms short-read and even some other long-read platforms on such samples; also a weak fit for very high-sample-count, shallow-depth applications (e.g., large-cohort NIPT, low-pass population genotyping) where short-read platforms' far lower cost/Gb dominates.

## Standardized scalability analysis

**Horizontal scaling**: PacBio's own generational roadmap (Sequel II → Sequel IIe → Revio) has been explicitly horizontal at the ZMW level — 8M ZMWs/cell (Sequel IIe) → 25M ZMWs/cell (Revio, a ~3× density increase) — plus increasing the number of SMRT Cells run simultaneously per instrument (1 → 4). This is the primary lever PacBio has used to scale output.

- **Bases per active ZMW per second (calculated from reported data)**: using the SPRQ-Nx generation's ~360 Gb per SMRT Cell over a 24 h run, and assuming (medium-low confidence, no independently sourced occupancy figure found this pass) roughly 60% of the 25M ZMWs are productively loaded and read-out (~15M productive ZMWs) — 360×10⁹ bases ÷ 15×10⁶ ZMWs ÷ 86,400 s ≈ **0.28 bases/productive ZMW/second**, averaged over the whole run. This is far lower than the polymerase's instantaneous single-molecule incorporation rate (historically reported in the low single digits of bases/second in earlier PacBio literature) because the averaged figure folds in loading inefficiency, non-productive ZMWs, and reads that terminate early — **label: calculated from reported data, medium-low confidence given the unverified occupancy assumption.**
- **Instruments needed for 1 Tb/day**: at SPRQ-Nx-generation throughput (~1.4 Tb/instrument/day, 4 cells × 360 Gb), **under 1 instrument** is needed nominally. At launch-chemistry throughput (~480 Gb/instrument/day), **~2.1 instruments** are needed. **Calculated from reported data**, assumes continuous back-to-back runs (theoretical-maximum utilization, not realistic).
- **Instruments needed for 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 human genomes/year**: PacBio's own marketing figure for Revio is **~2,500 genomes/year** at launch-generation chemistry (a lower, ~1,300 genomes/year figure was also reported at initial announcement in 2022, before chemistry improvements — **conflicting figures; the higher number reflects a later chemistry/software revision, treat the 1,300 figure as historical/superseded**). At 90% realistic uptime, ~2,250 genomes/year/instrument. **1 instrument comfortably covers 100 and 1,000 genomes/year; ~5 instruments are needed for 10,000 genomes/year** (10,000 ÷ 2,250 ≈ 4.4, rounded up). **Calculated from reported data**, medium confidence, and sensitive to which chemistry generation and coverage depth (20× vs. 30×) the "genomes/year" figure assumes — PacBio's own figures do not consistently specify this.

**Vertical scaling**: PacBio's gains have come more from **reducing the number of passes needed for a given consensus accuracy** (chemistry/polymerase engineering) and from **reusable SMRT Cells** (SPRQ-Nx) than from literally speeding up the polymerase's real-time incorporation rate, which is fundamentally bounded by single-molecule enzyme kinetics — a more vertically-constrained scaling path than horizontally adding ZMWs.

- **Primary physical bottleneck**: ZMW areal density is limited by diffraction/nanofabrication constraints — neighboring ZMWs must stay far enough apart to avoid optical crosstalk between adjacent fluorescence signals, and by the real-time polymerase incorporation rate itself, which (unlike a cyclic platform) cannot be sped up by shortening a wash/image cycle, since there is no such cycle to shorten. The fixed ~24 h run length is a deliberate tradeoff between more passes (higher per-molecule consensus accuracy, more data) and faster turnaround, not an incidental limitation.

## Competitive positioning

Revio competes directly with Oxford Nanopore's PromethION line for long-read WGS, de novo assembly, and structural-variant/phasing workloads — same customer (genome centers, population-genomics programs, rare-disease research groups), same budget line, same "do I need a long-read instrument at all" purchasing decision. Within that comparison, Revio's consensus-accuracy advantage (HiFi ~99.9%+ vs. ONT's raw/duplex accuracy — see [[Nanopore Strand Sequencing]] for ONT's own accuracy figures and the raw-vs-consensus distinction) is the primary technical differentiator PacBio markets against ONT, while ONT counters with lower instrument cost, portability, and native RNA sequencing (PacBio has no direct-RNA product; Iso-Seq sequences cDNA, not native RNA). Revio does **not** meaningfully compete with short-read platforms (Illumina NovaSeq X, MGI T7, Element AVITI) for high-sample-count, cost-sensitive SNV-calling workloads — those remain a different budget line and a different primary purchasing criterion (cost/genome for variant calling vs. structural/phasing completeness). Full cross-company competitive-landscape tables are consolidated in [[Instrument & Market Comparison]].
